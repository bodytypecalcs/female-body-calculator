      The 9 female body shapes

      Each silhouette below is drawn from typical measurements for that shape. Read the short description – then follow the links for a full styling guide on each body shape.

      Hourglass

      Bust and hips within about an inch of each other and a waist at least 9–10 inches smaller. Curves are balanced top and bottom.

      hourglass body shape

      Top Hourglass

      A clearly defined waist with a bust 1–10 inches larger than the hips. A variation within the hourglass family.

      Bottom Hourglass

      A clearly defined waist with hips 3.6–10 inches larger than the bust, widening gradually below the waist.

      Spoon

      Hips at least 2 inches larger than the bust with a distinct “shelf” at the high hip, just below the waist.

      spoon body shape

      Pear

      Hips 3.6–7 inches larger than the bust, narrower shoulders and a defined waist. One of the most common shapes.

      Triangle

      Hips 7 inches or more larger than the bust, with a moderately defined waist – much fuller below the waist than above.

      Apple

      Bust and hips similar, with a waist close to both. Fullness sits around the middle, often with slimmer legs.

      Commonly confused

      Pear vs triangle vs spoon – how to tell them apart

      All three have hips wider than the bust, which is why they are mixed up so often. The difference lies in how much wider the hips are and where the width begins.

      Get it right

      How to measure for an accurate result

      A difference of one inch can move you from one shape to the next, so take a minute to measure carefully. Stand in front of a mirror in your underwear or thin, close-fitting clothes, with your feet together and your arms relaxed.

      1. Bust: around the fullest part, wearing a non-padded bra.
      2. Waist: the narrowest point, usually just above the belly button – breathe out first.
      3. High hip: around the top of the hip bones, about 7 in (18 cm) below the waist.
      4. Hips: the widest part of the hips and seat, keeping the tape level.
      Pro tip

      Measure twice and use the average. If two readings differ by more than half an inch, check that the tape is level at the back – a slanted tape is the most common cause of a wrong result.

      Over time

      Can your body type change?

      Weight changes

      Gaining or losing weight changes circumferences, but not always evenly – many women keep the same basic shape at different sizes.

      Pregnancy

      Hips and bust often change during and after pregnancy. Wait a few months after birth before re-measuring.

      Menopause

      Hormonal changes can move fat storage towards the waist, which may shift a result towards rectangle or apple.

      Training

      Upper-body or glute training can add muscle where you train most, gently changing your proportions.

      FAQ

      Female body type questions

      How many female body types are there?

      This calculator uses nine female body shapes: hourglass, top hourglass, bottom hourglass, spoon, pear, triangle, apple, rectangle and inverted triangle. Many simpler systems combine them into five main families.

      What is the difference between pear and triangle body shape?

      Both have hips wider than the bust. In a pear shape the hips are about 3.6 to 7 inches larger with a defined waist; in a triangle shape the difference is 7 inches or more and the waist is less defined.

      Why is my high hip measurement needed?

      The high hip shows whether your hips widen gradually or form a shelf just below the waist. A high hip of 1.193 times your waist or more points to a spoon shape rather than a bottom hourglass.

      Can I be two body types at once?

      Measurements often sit close to a border between two shapes. The calculator follows a fixed order and gives the first shape whose rule matches, but if you are near a boundary, the neighbouring shape’s styling tips may suit you too.

      Does body type change after pregnancy or with age?

      Yes, proportions can shift with pregnancy, menopause, age and changes in weight or fitness. Re-measuring once in a while keeps your result up to date.
